# English-Native Writing Guide (Phase 14)

The posts are written in English for a Hungarian SME-owner audience. English is used deliberately
for authority/prestige positioning. Write like a confident, native-level English business voice —
NOT translated from Hungarian, and NOT generic LinkedIn-guru filler.

## Banned buzzwords / hype (never use)

leverage, revolutionize / revolutionary, game changer, seamless / seamlessly, disruptive /
disruption, cutting-edge, unlock your potential, synergy, supercharge, paradigm shift,
world-class, best-in-class, harness the power of, move the needle, low-hanging fruit,
"take it to the next level", "unlock", "elevate your", "empower".

Say the concrete thing instead. "We cut their reporting time from 6 hours to 20 minutes" beats
"we revolutionized their reporting workflow."

## AI-tells / stiff essay connectors (avoid)

"it's important to note", "in today's fast-paced world", "in today's digital age",
"at the end of the day", "when it comes to", "in conclusion", "furthermore", "moreover",
"delve into", "navigating the", "in the realm of", "a testament to", "tapestry",
"ever-evolving landscape", "let's dive in", "buckle up", "rest assured".

These read as ChatGPT/press-release English. Real operators don't write "furthermore" — they
start a new sentence, or use "and", "but", "so".

## Rhythm — native, not translated, not guru-spam

- Do NOT translate Hungarian sentence structure literally. Write the idea the way a native
  English speaker would say it out loud.
- Do NOT default to the "one punchy line. Then another. Then another." LinkedIn-guru cadence with
  no substance. Vary sentence length. A confident operator writes in real paragraphs and uses
  short lines only for genuine emphasis.
- Contractions are fine and natural (we've, don't, here's). Stiff full forms read as translated.

## Voice stays intact

English does NOT mean corporate. David still sounds like a hands-on engineer; Adam still sounds
like a sharp operator talking owner-to-owner; PlanSmart still sounds like a precise, human B2B
brand. English is the language, not a personality change.

## Test yourself

Read it out loud. If it sounds like a smart founder talking to another founder over coffee, it's
right. If it sounds like a press release, a translated post, or a LinkedIn-guru thread, rewrite it.
